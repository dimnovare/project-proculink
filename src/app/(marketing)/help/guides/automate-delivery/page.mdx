<Prereqs>
- At least one order for this supplier taken to supplier-ready and checked by you — see [take your first
  order to supplier-ready](/help/guides/first-order-end-to-end). Automate what you have already seen work.
- From the supplier, in writing: **how** they want orders sent, **in what format**, and the credentials for
  it.
- The right plan for the channel they choose. Email, SFTP and FTPS delivery are on every plan, Pilot
  included, in any format except cXML. Posting to an HTTP endpoint is {requiresPlan("webhookDelivery")},
  cXML output is {requiresPlan("cxml")}, and the Erply/Directo ERP adapters are
  {requiresPlan("erpConnectors")}.
</Prereqs>

<Step n={1} title="Ask the supplier how they want orders">

This is the step with a person in it, so start it early — the answer can take a day.

Ask them three questions and write the answers down:

1. **How should we send orders?** An email address, an SFTP or FTPS folder, or an endpoint we post to?
2. **In what format?** CSV, JSON, XML, cXML, UBL 2.1, or X12 850?
3. **What credentials do we need,** and who do we tell when a test arrives?

Ask all three regardless of your plan — you want the real answer on file. But check what your plan can save
before you promise it: an HTTP endpoint needs {requiresPlan("webhookDelivery")} and cXML output needs
{requiresPlan("cxml")}. ProcuLink does not send PDF or Excel files, so a supplier who only takes those keeps
getting your downloaded file by hand, as in the first-order guide.

</Step>

<Step n={2} title="Set up the channel on the Delivery tab">

Open **Suppliers → the supplier → Delivery**. A supplier with nothing saved yet also gets an offer to walk
you through it step by step; either way ends in the same saved setup.

Pick the channel the supplier named:

- **Email** — fill in **Recipients (comma-separated)**; a subject and body template are optional. It goes
  out over ProcuLink's own mail servers.
- **SFTP** or **FTPS** — the **Host**, **Port**, and the **Remote path** the file is written to, signed in
  by password or private key.
- **HTTP** — the **Endpoint URL** (an `https://` address), the method, and the sign-in the supplier
  specified.

Then set **Output format — the format this supplier requires**, and press **Save delivery**. Field by
field, per channel, including cXML and the ERP adapters: [set up delivery to a supplier and test-fire
it](/help/guides/set-up-supplier-delivery).

Saving does not change how orders are sent. Every supplier starts on **Review before delivery**, and stays
there until step 5.

<IfThisFails>
A save refused with a plan message means the channel or format is not on your plan — the refusal comes when
you save, not later when an order is sent.
</IfThisFails>

</Step>

<Step n={3} title="Test the connection">

Press **Test connection**, or **Send a test now** on the strip that appears after saving. It stays disabled
until the setup is saved, because the test runs against what is stored.

The test sends a small sample file, not an order — but it does reach the supplier, so tell them first. The
panel says beforehand what it leaves behind:

- **Email** — a real email in their inbox, titled *ProcuLink connection test — this is not a purchase order*.
- **SFTP / FTPS** — a file called `proculink-test.csv` in their folder, which stays there until someone
  deletes it.
- **HTTP** — a real request to their live endpoint, which their system may keep a record of.

**The supplier's system answered** means the path and the credentials work. It does not mean an order would
be accepted — their own rules about item codes and fields still apply.

<IfThisFails>
**We could not reach the supplier's system** is the address or a firewall, not the sign-in. A message that
names a response code says what refused — the sign-in, the address, or the request — and, when they gave
a reason, **Their system said:** quotes it. Fix what it names and test again.
</IfThisFails>

</Step>

<Step n={4} title="Send one real order yourself">

Open a reviewed order for this supplier and press **Send to supplier**. The confirmation names where it is
going; confirm, and the order moves through **Sending** to **Delivered**.

Then do the thing that is easy to skip: **ask the supplier whether it arrived and looks right.**
**Delivered** means their system accepted the transmission — not that a person or an ERP on their side
accepted the order. For a first send, that gap is exactly where surprises live.

<IfThisFails>
A send that fails shows **Couldn't send** with the reason captured, and can be retried once the cause is
fixed. Statuses and what each one leads to: [order statuses from upload to
delivered](/help/dashboard-and-statuses).
</IfThisFails>

</Step>

<Step n={5} title="Choose review before delivery or automatic delivery">

The switch is at the top of the supplier's **Delivery** tab, beside **Supplier delivery**. It has two
settings, and the line under the heading names the one you are on:

- **Review before delivery** — the default. An order whose output has been prepared waits as **Queued to
  send** until someone sends it.
- **Automatic delivery** — tick it and press **Save delivery**. An order is sent as soon as its output is
  prepared, without a further click.

**Send to supplier** on the review screen sends straight away under either setting; the switch decides what
happens to an order that was prepared without one, such as with **Prepare output** in the order queue. An
order with an item code still open cannot be prepared, so it is never sent automatically.

Stay on review before delivery until a run of orders for this supplier has gone out without a correction.
Switching back is the same box, unticked and saved. The **Suppliers** list shows each supplier's delivery
mode, so you can see at a glance which ones send on their own.

<Outcome>
Orders for this supplier reach them through ProcuLink, and you chose — knowingly — whether each one waits for
you first.
</Outcome>

</Step>

<Step n={6} title="Stop uploading by hand">

With delivery settled, the other half of the manual work is getting orders in. Pick the channel that matches
how orders already reach you and set it up once:

- Orders arrive **by email** → [receive orders by email](/help/guides/receive-orders-by-email)
- A system can **push structured data** → [push orders in over the
  API](/help/guides/receive-orders-over-api)
- A system **exports files to a folder** → [pull order files from an SFTP
  folder](/help/guides/receive-orders-by-sftp) or [from S3 or Cloudflare
  R2](/help/guides/receive-orders-from-s3)

The API is on every plan, as manual upload is. The email, SFTP and S3 routes are
{requiresPlan("emailIngestion", "sftpIngestion", "s3Ingestion")}.

They all land in the same queue and are reviewed exactly as an upload is. Only the way in changes.

<Outcome>
Orders arrive on their own, and your job narrows to the ones that genuinely need a decision.
</Outcome>

</Step>
