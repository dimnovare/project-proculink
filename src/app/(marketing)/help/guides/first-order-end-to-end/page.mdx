<Prereqs>
- A ProcuLink workspace you can sign in to. Pilot reaches **delivered** by SFTP, FTPS or email, in any
  format except cXML. The other two choices in step 5 are behind a plan: posting to an HTTP endpoint is
  {requiresPlan("webhookDelivery")}, cXML output is {requiresPlan("cxml")}, and the Erply/Directo ERP
  adapters are {requiresPlan("erpConnectors")}. Everything before step 5 — upload, review, item codes — is
  the same on Pilot.
- **One real purchase order** you have actually sent, or would send. Not a sample — a sample proves the plumbing and teaches you nothing about your own documents.
- One supplier you send to often, and their contact details.
- Around an hour, though most of it is waiting on the supplier to tell you how they want orders delivered.
</Prereqs>

<Step n={1} title="Add the supplier">

Everything else hangs off this. ProcuLink checks item codes against **a** supplier, delivers to **a**
supplier, and routes incoming orders to **a** supplier — so one has to exist before anything can happen.

**Partners → Suppliers → New supplier**, name it, save. That is the required part, and it takes a minute.

While you are there, fill in the **Identifiers** card on the supplier's Overview tab — their VAT number,
registration number, EDI/GLN code, and email domain. It is optional, and it is what lets a future order
that does not name them plainly still find its way. Full detail: [add a supplier and fill in their
identifiers](/help/guides/add-a-supplier).

<Outcome>
The supplier appears in **Partners → Suppliers**.
</Outcome>

</Step>

<Step n={2} title="Load their item codes">

Ask the supplier for their current price list as a CSV or XLSX, and import it on the supplier's **Catalog**
tab.

This step is optional and skipping it is the single most common reason a first order feels like hard work.
With a catalog, unknown codes are flagged and suggestions come from products the supplier really sells.
Without one, every unfamiliar code on the order is something you resolve by hand with nothing to check
against.

Ten minutes here saves most of step 4. See [import a supplier catalog from a
file](/help/guides/import-a-supplier-catalog).

<IfThisFails>
No price list to hand? Carry on. Every code you resolve manually is remembered, so the mapping table fills
itself from real work — it is just slower to start.
</IfThisFails>

</Step>

<Step n={3} title="Upload a real purchase order">

Open **Upload**, set **Send to** to your supplier, drop the file, and press **↑ Upload & review**.

CSV, XLSX, and text-based PDF are the everyday formats; structured XML, cXML, UBL, EDIFACT, X12, and SAP
IDoc are read too. Each file becomes its own order — files are never merged.

ProcuLink parses in the background and opens the review screen when it is ready. More on the upload screen
itself: [upload order files by hand](/help/guides/upload-orders-manually).

<Outcome>
The order opens on its own review screen, with the right purchase-order number and its line items.
</Outcome>

<IfThisFails>
An order that stops at **Failed** is a document problem rather than a ProcuLink one — most often a missing
required column, a scanned PDF with no text layer, or an encoding that mangled the header row. See
[troubleshooting common parse errors](/help/troubleshooting).
</IfThisFails>

</Step>

<Step n={4} title="Clear the review list">

The review screen shows **What we received** on the left, **What we'll send** in the middle, and a panel on
the right with two tabs: **Issues** — the **Before you send** list — and **Preview**, the document your
supplier would actually receive.

Work the Issues list. Only entries marked **BLOCKER** stop a send; **WARNING** entries are worth a look but
do not hold anything up. For each unresolved item code, either **Accept suggestion** — with its confidence
shown — or **Enter manually** and type the supplier's own code.

Everything you resolve here is remembered per supplier, so this list gets shorter with every order. Two
guides go deeper: [review an order and send it](/help/guides/review-an-order) and [resolve item codes in
review](/help/guides/resolve-item-codes).

<Outcome>
The panel reads **Ready to send · No open issues. This is everything ProcuLink can check before sending — it
is not a guarantee that the supplier will accept the order.**
</Outcome>

</Step>

<Step n={5} title="Ask the supplier how they want it, then configure delivery">

This is the step with a person in it, so start it early — often before step 3, since the answer can take a
day.

Ask them three questions and write the answers down:

1. **How should we send orders?** An endpoint we post to, an SFTP folder, or an email address?
2. **In what format?** CSV, JSON, XML, cXML, UBL/Peppol, or X12 850?
3. **What credentials do we need,** and who do we tell when a test arrives?

Ask all three regardless of your plan — you want the real answer on file. But check what your plan can save
before you promise it: an HTTP endpoint needs {requiresPlan("webhookDelivery")}, cXML output needs
{requiresPlan("cxml")}, and Erply/Directo need {requiresPlan("erpConnectors")}. SFTP, FTPS and email, in any
format but cXML, are on every plan including Pilot — so a Pilot workspace can finish this guide by agreeing
one of those with the supplier.

Then fill that into the supplier's **Delivery** tab, press **Save delivery**, and press **Test-fire**. A
green result proves the credentials and the path against their real endpoint before any order depends on
it. Full walkthrough, per channel: [set up delivery to a supplier and test-fire
it](/help/guides/set-up-supplier-delivery).

<IfThisFails>
Until delivery is configured, sending stops at *delivery not set up*. That is deliberate — an order is never
reported as sent when nothing has been sent. Everything up to that point still works, so you can review
orders today and configure delivery when the supplier answers.
</IfThisFails>

</Step>

<Step n={6} title="Send it, and check what the supplier saw">

Send the order. It moves `ready_to_deliver` → `delivering` → `delivered`.

Then do the thing that is easy to skip on a first order: **ask the supplier whether it arrived and looks
right.** `delivered` means their system accepted the transmission — not that a person or an ERP on their
side accepted the order. For a first send, that gap is exactly where surprises live.

<Outcome>
One real purchase order has gone the whole way through ProcuLink and been confirmed by the supplier — and
the item codes, the mapping, and the delivery configuration you set up along the way are reused by every
order after it.
</Outcome>

</Step>

<Step n={7} title="Stop uploading by hand">

You have now done the whole path manually once, which is the right way to learn it and the wrong way to run
it.

Pick the channel that matches how orders already reach you and set it up once:

- Orders arrive **by email** → [receive orders by email](/help/guides/receive-orders-by-email)
- A system can **push structured data** → [push orders in over the
  API](/help/guides/receive-orders-over-api)
- A system **exports files to a folder** → [pull order files from an SFTP
  folder](/help/guides/receive-orders-by-sftp) or [from S3 or Cloudflare
  R2](/help/guides/receive-orders-from-s3)

They all land in the same Inbox and are reviewed exactly as you just did. Only the way in changes.

<Outcome>
Orders arrive on their own, and your job narrows to the ones that genuinely need a decision.
</Outcome>

</Step>
